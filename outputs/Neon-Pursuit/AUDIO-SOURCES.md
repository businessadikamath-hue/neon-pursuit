# v8 engine synthesis: source evidence and provenance

All engine audio remains original procedural synthesis. No manufacturer audio, YouTube sound, field recording, commercial sample pack, or other third-party recording was downloaded or copied. The four existing 660-second biome recordings are preserved byte-for-byte.

The references below establish engine families and selected operating/acoustic characteristics. They do **not** establish the exact frequency response of the synthesizer. Harmonic weights, exhaust/intake resonances, cylinder amplitude differences and mix levels are authored approximations. In particular, the modeled pulse-weight arrays are not presented as measured firing orders or a physical exhaust-manifold simulation.

## Synthesis method

The internal-combustion waveform spans a full 720-degree four-stroke cycle. Its base repetition frequency is RPM divided by 120; the cylinder count determines how many pressure pulses appear in that cycle. A damped asymmetric pulse and weaker delayed pressure return create the original harmonic coefficients. WebAudio's periodic-wave implementation uses 192 harmonics, and separate exhaust, intake and low-order body tables pass through filters that respond to RPM and load.

Low-level filtered air/mechanical noise and small continuous frequency/amplitude variations add texture. Turbo air is present only on the boosted combustion profiles. These are compact waveform definitions generated locally at runtime; they do not require new downloaded samples or runtime network access. The existing dynamics compressor and oversampled soft limiter remain after the live engine/effect mix.

## Shift contract

A distinct shift transient is eligible only for an actual upward gear change while vehicle audio and driving are active, the driver is pressing the accelerator, the car is not braking or in sustained deceleration, and the car is not electric. Any downshift cancels the transient. Coasting, braking, deceleration and pedal release cancel scheduled transient audio immediately. Car changes, first powertrain initialization and backwards simulation-clock resets initialize silently.

The source accepts `acceleration` in mph per second, `throttleAmount` as actual powertrain throttle, and `driverThrottle` as driver intent. If omitted, it derives speed change from speed/time and uses the existing throttle argument. Engine load can still reflect cruise control while driver-pedal shift effects remain suppressed.

RPM changes can still alter the continuous engine note during real downshifts; that is separate from the disabled shift burst and disabled downshift sound cut. A short torque interruption during a legitimate accelerator-held upshift can produce slightly negative instantaneous acceleration. The source recognizes that narrow case only when the immediately preceding update had positive driver-powered acceleration and the gear increases while the shift flag is active. The allowance lasts no longer than the car's shift time plus a small margin; release, braking, a downshift or the end of the shift cancels it. Sustained deceleration cannot open the allowance. Diagnostics report raw negative acceleration and recognized powered shift interruption separately.

## Limits

This is an original engine-family approximation. It is not an authentic recording, calibrated acoustic model, or guarantee of real-car fidelity on a particular speaker. The literature establishes mechanical characteristics; perceived sound depends heavily on exhaust geometry, microphone/listener position, load, materials, filtering and playback equipment that are simplified here. Waveform/output tests establish activity, difference and gating behavior, not subjective parity with a real vehicle.
